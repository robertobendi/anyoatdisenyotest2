# ANALYSIS

## Business
ANYO at DISENYO is an architectural services firm based in Batangas City,
Philippines, operating primarily through its Facebook page
(facebook.com/anyoatdisenyo). The name is Filipino for "Form and Design,"
and the firm offers architectural design and related services — most
plausibly residential and small-commercial design, planning, and possibly
construction supervision for local clients. As a small regional design
practice it makes money through project-based design fees and service
engagements (consultation, plans, project development), won largely through
local reputation, referrals, and its social presence. The public footprint
is modest (~281 page likes), so this is a small, owner-led practice rather
than a large corporate firm.

## Audience
- **Primary**: Batangas-area homeowners and small property/business owners,
  roughly 30–55, who are about to build or renovate and are looking for a
  trustworthy local architect to turn an idea (a home, a shop, an addition)
  into buildable plans. In the moment they want to see that this firm is
  real, competent, and reachable — and to message or call to start a project.
- **Secondary**: Contractors and engineers who partner on builds, returning
  past clients, suppliers, and prospective hires/interns; occasionally
  out-of-town family members financing a relative's house.

## What they're trying to convey
- Competent, credentialed local professionals — "real architects, not just
  a draftsman" — trustworthy with a serious investment.
- Design-led and modern, but grounded and approachable for everyday Filipino
  homeowners, not an elite metro-Manila luxury studio.
- An established, here-to-stay practice (named firm, logo, location) rather
  than a fly-by-night freelancer.

## What's broken about the current presentation
- Lives entirely on a Facebook page — no owned website, so there is no
  controllable hero, narrative, or conversion path; anonymous visitors hit a
  cookie/login wall instead of the brand.
- No portfolio showcase: an architecture firm's single strongest proof —
  photos of completed projects — is invisible to a first-time visitor.
- No clear call-to-action or contact funnel; phone, email, hours, and
  service list are not surfaced anywhere accessible.
- Visual identity is reduced to a profile-picture logo; no typography,
  layout, or photography system expresses the design competence the firm
  sells (an architect with no design on its own page undercuts the pitch).
- No trust signals — no credentials/PRC license mention, no testimonials,
  no project count, no "about the principal."

## Primary objective
Get a qualified local prospect to make first contact (message/call) to start
a project. Secondary: showcase a credible portfolio of completed work, and
establish that this is an established, licensed, here-to-stay practice.

  "a" and "d" (for **A**nyo **D**isenyo) that link into a single

  Next stages should not invent post copy or a palette from these scraps.

## Voice
Assured, warm, professional. Writes like a competent local architect
speaking plainly to a homeowner about to make a big investment — confident
about craft and design, but grounded, friendly, and free of jargon or
luxury-brand pretension. The Filipino name ("Form and Design") invites a
bilingual, regionally-rooted warmth rather than cold corporate polish.

## Constraints the next stage must respect
- **Brand color is locked to the olive/moss green** of the existing
  monogram (~`#6e7044`); the palette must build around an earthy
  green/taupe family, not the Facebook blues in `palette.json`.
- **The interlocking "ad" loop monogram and the "ANYO at DISENYO /
  ARCHITECTURAL SERVICES" lock-up must be preserved** as the logo.
- **User references lead the aesthetic** (see REFERENCES.md): both refs are
  warm, editorial, architecture-studio sites on cream/off-white grounds with
  serif-display + clean-sans pairings, big architectural photography, and
  editorially-set stats — this direction is strongly implied and aligns with
  the firm's olive/earth brand color. Avoid cold tech-blue, brutalist, or
  playful/Y2K families — they conflict with both the brand mark and the refs.
- **Portfolio + contact conversion are functional must-haves** the layout
  has to make room for, regardless of aesthetic.
